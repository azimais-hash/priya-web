A specialism within the practice

British South Asian women at a midlife crossroads

You did everything that was asked of you. This is where you get to ask something of yourself.

You are usually the one holding it together. At work, where you have built something substantial. At home, where you are daughter, wife, mother, sister, and the person who remembers everyone’s appointments and everyone’s feelings. In the community, where coping well is its own quiet currency.

Underneath the competence, a question has started to surface. After all of this, what do I want the next ten or fifteen years to be?

It rarely arrives on its own. It comes with a milestone birthday, a parent’s illness, a child leaving home, a marriage at a crossroads, or a perimenopause nobody around you seems willing to name.

What often sits underneath

  • Belonging without becoming. You love where you come from, and it left very little room for you.
  • The good girl shadow. Years of being agreeable, accommodating and careful never to make a fuss.
  • Success without satisfaction. You achieved everything that was expected, and it still feels flat.
  • Relational overextension. Children, parents, in-laws, community. All of them real, all of them yours.
  • The identity vacuum. The structures that gave your life its shape no longer hold it.

What this work is

This is about finding yourself inside your culture.

Nothing here asks you to step away from your heritage, your family or your faith. A great deal of what you carry is genuinely yours and worth keeping. The work is telling the difference between what you chose and what you absorbed, and then deciding what the next part of your life gets built on.

Culture explains part of this. It never explains all of you. You will not spend our sessions being reduced to your background, and you will not spend them explaining it either.

Questions worth sitting with

  • Whose expectations am I still organising my life around?
  • What did I agree to in my twenties that I would not agree to now?
  • How do I hold a boundary with people I love without it landing as rejection?
  • What do I want the second half to be for?

You do not need answers to these. Notice which one you hesitated over.

How we work together

This is private one to one coaching, held with the same rigour as the rest of the practice. Most women start with The Reclamation Programme: six months, twelve fortnightly sessions, and the five phases of Recognise, Release, Reclaim, Redesign and Rise.

Cultural fluency is simply part of the room. The duty, the silence around menopause, the weight of being the dependable one, the complicated love of a family you would not trade. You can begin in the middle of the story and I will follow you.

Why me
